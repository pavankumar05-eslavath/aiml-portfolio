### Reading these tables

`R@k` is bounded above by `k / |relevant|`. Relevant sets here hold tens of products,
so recall at small `k` cannot approach 1.0 and is useful only for *comparing rows*,
never as an absolute score. `nDCG@10` and `MRR` are the metrics to compare on.

## Summary of findings

1. For **text** queries the same-modality channel (text->text) scores nDCG@10 0.572 versus 0.514 cross-modal; for **image** queries image->image scores 0.796 versus 0.818. The cross-modal channel is competitive, so it earns a meaningful share of the weight.
2. Multimodal weighting is **query-dependent**: contradiction-style queries peak at `image=0.10 / text=0.90` (nDCG@10 0.429) while agreement-style queries peak at `image=0.50 / text=0.50` (nDCG@10 0.831). Weighting the image heavily pulls results back toward the attribute a contradiction query asked to change, so the weights are exposed per request.
3. The best cross-modal weight on single-modality queries is **0.30** (nDCG@10 0.754).
4. At a matched image share, the best fusion strategy on multimodal queries is **weighted_sum (image share 0.3)** (nDCG@10 0.588).
5. Normalisation choice: **zscore** performs best (nDCG@10 0.694).
6. Lexical channel: **0.00** is best (nDCG@10 0.677).
7. Increasing top_k from 20 to 100 changes nDCG@10 from 0.683 to 0.690 while median latency moves from 45.5ms to 67.5ms.

## Experiment 2 - image/text weighting for multimodal queries

Multimodal queries split into two kinds that pull in opposite directions,
so they are swept separately. Averaging them would make any recommended
weight an artefact of how many of each the benchmark happens to contain.

- **contradiction** - the text names an attribute the image does *not* have
  (a white shoe plus "but in black"); relevance requires the attribute from
  the **text**.
- **agreement** - the text restates what the image already shows; relevance
  requires the shared attributes.

### The two groups disagree

| Query group   | Best weighting         | Best nDCG@10 | nDCG@10 at 50/50 |
|---------------|------------------------|--------------|------------------|
| contradiction | image=0.10 / text=0.90 | 0.429        | 0.279            |
| agreement     | image=0.50 / text=0.50 | 0.831        | 0.831            |

No single fixed weighting is optimal for both, which is why
`IMAGE_WEIGHT`/`TEXT_WEIGHT` are request-overridable rather than
compile-time constants: a UI can expose the trade-off, and the
environment default only has to be a sane midpoint.

## Experiment 4 - score fusion vs embedding fusion

`weighted_sum` and `rrf` retrieve from each channel separately and fuse *scores*. `embedding_fusion` blends the image and text query vectors into one vector *before* retrieval - the approach that first comes to mind, and the one that cannot attribute a result to a modality afterwards.

Each strategy is run at a **matched image share** (`image_weight` for the score-level strategies, `alpha` for embedding fusion) so the comparison isolates the fusion mechanism rather than rewarding a luckier weighting.

|--------------------------------|----|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|---------|
| weighted_sum (image share 0.3) | 20 | 0.777 | 0.433 | 0.165 | 0.268 | 0.428 | 0.640 | 0.550 | 0.588   |
| rrf (image share 0.3)          | 20 | 0.752 | 0.417 | 0.158 | 0.258 | 0.428 | 0.620 | 0.540 | 0.575   |
